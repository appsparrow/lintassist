The part no one talks about

Most ADA reviews happen after the design feels finished.

By then, a contrast issue is not just a contrast issue. It is a ticket, a review comment, a rework loop, and a delay. The same thing happens with basic heuristics and Gestalt principles—not because designers do not know them, but because applying theory to every screen at deadline speed is nearly impossible.

01 · BEFORE THE REVIEW

“What did I miss?”

You want a fast outside perspective before you walk into critique—not a replacement for your judgment, just a way to arrive better prepared.

02 · BEFORE ADA

“Can I catch this earlier?”

You would rather spot obvious contrast, hierarchy, and accessibility risks while the screen is still easy to change.

03 · UNDER DEADLINE

“Is this actually clear?”

You are moving quickly, and the screen looks polished. A quiet second opinion helps reveal what familiarity is hiding.

Common questions

What to know before your first UX review.

01

What does LintAssist review?

It gives AI-assisted feedback on a screen’s usability, visual hierarchy, Gestalt grouping, contrast, and accessibility concerns, with findings you can consider fixing.

02

Does it replace an accessibility audit?

No. The feedback is a starting point, not a compliance determination. AI can make mistakes, so validate findings with accessibility expertise and appropriate testing.

03

How do I use it?

Start with a screenshot in the web app. LintAssist also supports reviewing Figma frames with its plugin and visible pages with its Chrome extension.

04

Can I try it free?

Yes. Start with two audits without creating an account. The app explains the available access options when you reach the free limit.
