# Inspirational / product-free version

For audiences who should feel the story without learning what was built (internal motivation, external curiosity).

**Length:** 700–900 words. Warm, direct, second-person invitation near the end.

## Hard rule
**Never reveal the product.** No name, no platform names, no feature specifics, no domain, no screenshots. Describe things generically: "a small tool", "the people around me", "an environment that behaved differently than my laptop".

## Structure
1. **Title.** A line that implies persistence. ("The idea that wouldn't go away.")
2. **Bold subtitle** with the arc: small idea, a few weekends, everything between "someone should build this" and "it's live".
3. **Opening scene.** A Tuesday-at-work moment: a familiar snag, the thought *someone should build something for this*, and how it normally fades.
4. **Sections** (short, 3–5 sentences each):
   1. Notice what repeats
   2. Give it a weekend, not a roadmap
   3. It's fine if the idea goes to sleep (and keeps pulling you back)
   4. Small is the right size
   5. Walls are the curriculum
   6. Then it becomes a system (access, limits, trust, documentation, story)
   7. AI changes the volume, not the job
5. **"What I'd tell you"** bullet list.
6. **Closing question** the reader can act on. ("What's the problem you've noticed three times this month? That's probably your weekend.")

## Rules
- The emotional core is persistence and permission: ordinary people can start, and returning to an idea is a strength.
- Keep the AI/human split ("AI carries volume; humans carry judgment").
- No asking for anything from the reader or the organisation.
