01 · The observation

Everyone was building. I could see what was missing.

Working inside product teams, the gap was not effort or tooling. It was a fast, structured second opinion before a review. Not a verdict. A lens.

A
“I know something feels off, but I don’t know where to start.”

A score and ranked findings give a first move.

B
“I need a second pair of eyes before the review.”

Nielsen, Gestalt and accessibility checks as a baseline.

C
“The feedback lives nowhere near my workflow.”

So the tool has to meet people where they already work.

02 · The pivot chain

Multi-channel delivery: web, Figma and Chrome.

I saw

Designers need an honest read on a screen, fast, without a full review.

I decided

Start with a screenshot-to-audit web app on real frameworks, not vague “looks nice” feedback.

It unlocked

Proof the idea worked, and a question: where do designers actually live?

I saw

Designers live in Figma. Exporting screenshots is friction.

I decided

Ship a plugin that audits the selected frame and places the report on the canvas. A sandbox wall forced a backend of our own.

It unlocked

A real API and a way to reach the Figma community.

I saw

Two surfaces should never mean two accounts or two sets of rules.

I decided

One token and one backend for every surface, with the same tokens carried across design and code.

It unlocked

A new surface became cheap. That is the decision everything after depended on.

I saw

A quick check should feel like a glance, not a workflow.

I decided

Collapse the whole interface into three steps: capture, choose a lens, read the report.

It unlocked

A consistent interaction model that carries to any new surface.

I saw

Not everything worth auditing lives in Figma. Live pages needed the same lens.

I decided

A Chrome side panel on the same backend and access model. Built and submitted in one weekend.

It unlocked

About 90 minutes from idea to a working package, now published.

05 · Hiccups

Two moments that shaped the product.

The wall

Figma’s sandbox blocked every call.

What happened
The plugin couldn’t reach the AI provider. Origin was null and the request was refused.
What I did
Put a small proxy at the edge, then made it the single backend for every surface.
What it became
The reason a third surface took a weekend, not a quarter.
The call

I held back payments that weren’t ready.

What happened
Paid plans were coded, but prices, links and webhooks weren’t live and tested.
What I did
Hid them and shipped optional Ko-fi support, with bonus audits granted automatically.
What it became
A trust-first free tier and an honest signal of who values it.

08 · The playbook

It isn’t one product. It’s a method other teams can run.

The same six moves, applied to any use case a team already feels.

The best ideas sit in the complaints teams have stopped hearing. Listen for them.

Who needs what, when, and why now. The technology is chosen last.

One access model and one source of truth make every new channel cheap.

Use real frameworks on the product itself, then fold the findings back in.

PRD, tasks, release notes and the story are part of the build, not an afterthought.

Taste, trust and the decision to cut. AI does the lifting; people own the call.
